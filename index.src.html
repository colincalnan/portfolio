Generative media SaaS

AdvisorAssist

On-brand social content, generated and published for every customer on a schedule, for financial advisors across Canada. An advisor paid through Stripe, connected their own accounts, and picked a tier. From there it ran itself: a scheduled emitter pulled that tier out of Airtable and fired one event per advisor, a listener assembled the post, composited it into a template using their own logo and their own three brand colours, and published it to their connected accounts on their scheduled date. Built in 2022, before there was a name for any of it.

Idea

An advisor's whole business is trust, and the thing that builds it is showing up in the feed every week. Almost none of them do, because writing is not the job they trained for. Sell them the showing up.

Brand

Named, drawn and priced. Logo, tagline, and three tiers, Engage, Elevate, Elite, each with its own rendered banner, because a tier nobody can picture does not sell.

Build

A scheduled emitter fires one event per advisor. Listeners assemble the post, composite it into a template with that advisor's own logo and brand colours, and publish through a webhook. v341.

Role
Built the product. A partner brought the advisor relationships.
Pipeline
Emitter and listener workflows on a monthly schedule, tier-gated features in code, per-advisor auth by single sign-on so nobody handed me a password, and a branded render step before publish.
The copy
Generated in batch, not per post. Two years of my partner's own writing as the voice, mapped to the seasons that move an advisor's year, run through the OpenAI API from inside Airtable.
Status
Ran live. Wound down when the partnership stalled, not when the product did. The publish workflow reached v341.

Two accounts, one pipeline

Wines and Tours of the Okanagan

Two Instagram accounts running the same machine on two different data sources. One scraped the provincial winery database through its API, generated the imagery and the copy, and posted unattended. It also read winery events out of an inbox and posted those, and ran wine scores for a while. The second did it again for wine tour reviews. Building it twice off different inputs is the part I care about: the second one was a configuration, not a rebuild.

Idea

Every winery in the province is already in a public database, and every one of them wants to be found. Nobody was turning that into a feed. So the source data was the content, and the only real question was the rendering.

Brand

Two accounts, two names, one visual system underneath. Template families rather than per-post design, which is the only way an unattended feed stays on brand.

Build

Scraped the source through its API, generated the imagery and copy, published on a schedule with nobody in the loop. Then pointed the same machine at tour reviews and it worked again.

Role
Everything. Scraper, generation, templates, scheduling, both accounts.
Approach
Structured source data in, generated on-brand posts out, published on a schedule with nobody in the loop. A template family rather than a run of one-offs.

Product, with payments

Set List Easy

A searchable, filterable set list of two, three and four chord songs for musicians who are starting to gig. The problem is real and specific: you can play six chords and you need two hours of material by Friday. Built, rebuilt, and taken to a mobile app, because the interesting question kept changing and the content model kept being right.

Idea

You can play six chords and you have a two hour gig on Friday. Every song list online is organised by artist or by decade. None of them are organised by the only thing that matters at that moment, which is how hard it is to play.

Brand

A pick and a wordmark, plus the whole kit around it: favicons, social tiles, ad creative. Built to look like a product because it charged like one.

Build

Search and filter over a curated content model, accounts, subscriptions and a perform mode for on stage. Four builds across its life, ending as a native mobile app with a real test suite.

Role
Everything. Product, content model, design, build, payments, brand.
Approach
Accounts and subscriptions from the start, so it had to be a product rather than a demo. Four builds across the life of it, ending in a native mobile app with a real test suite.

Live tool, on stage

Gig Pulse

An iPad app for the gig itself. Tap the energy in the room against each song as you play, build up a history across nights, then let a model read the history back and tell you which songs earn their place and where the set sags. Dark and high contrast because the only place it gets used is a dark room, mid-set, at arm's length.

Idea

Every musician thinks they know which songs land. Nobody writes it down, because you cannot take notes with a guitar on. Make it one tap, and the set list stops being a hunch.

Brand

Dark and high contrast, because the only place it gets used is a dark room, mid-set, at arm's length. That constraint decided most of the design before anything was drawn.

Build

A progressive web app so it installs without a store, with the model provider behind an abstraction rather than wired straight in. Every decision tested at a real gig that week.

Role
Everything, and I am the user. Every design decision was tested at a real gig that week.
Approach
Progressive web app so it installs without a store. Model provider behind an abstraction rather than wired straight in, so swapping one is a config change.

Safe Family Internet

A whole product built to get non-technical parents to actually change something at home. Identity, the live site, a gated download library of step-by-step guides, and the email pipeline behind it. The hard part was never the technology. It was making a frightening subject simple enough that a tired parent would act on it the same evening.

Idea

Parents are not short of warnings about the internet. They are short of one thing they can actually do tonight. So the whole product answers a single question: what do I change, and in what order.

Brand

A shield, a wordmark, and a mark that animates from unprotected to protected. Built so a volunteer could use it without me in the room.

Build

A static site on continuous deploy, a gated download library, and an email gate through a serverless function into the campaign platform. 260 sign-ups claimed every seat for one night in a town of 15,000.

Role
Everything. Brand, copy, design, build, deploy, and the talk it all supports.
Approach
Static site on a private repo, continuous deploy, an email gate wired through a serverless function into the campaign platform and its templates.
Result
260 sign-ups claimed every seat, 38 more on the waitlist, and 48 households ran a home network checkup. Covered by CBC Daybreak and AM1150.

Design system

Bold Talks, and Ground

A presentation system written as tokens first, then a deck built on top of it so the talk could not drift from the brand. Eight ground colours, one condensed display face, and two rules that settle most arguments before they start: no radii, no shadows. It has since become Ground, which turns the same decisions into something an agent can build against: semantic tokens so a page never names a colour, three themes it can flip between, a guidance document written as prose, and a check script that fails a build. The page you are reading is the first thing built on it.

Idea

A brand nobody can apply is not a brand. Write the decisions down as something a machine can check, and the rules survive contact with a deadline.

Brand
ground cobalt electric violet peach cyan orange yellow

Build

Semantic tokens so a page never names a colour, three themes it can flip between, guidance written as prose an agent reads, and a check script that fails a build.

Role
Designed the system, built the deck that consumes it, then rebuilt it as a token contract three surfaces now share.
Approach
Judgment written as prose an agent reads, reusable mechanics in the stylesheet, and anything a machine can decide turned into a check that can fail on its own.
Result
Sized in rem against a fixed canvas so type holds on a projector. Reused since across guides, carousels, the site and this page. Fourteen checks that each fail a build, from contrast to whether every number carries its source.

Generative media pipeline

Parent guides

Illustrated step-by-step guides for locking down a phone, a console or a router, built from screenshots I captured myself on the actual devices. A pipeline takes the raw captures, redacts anything personal, drops them into a black phone frame, highlights the exact control to tap in brand pink, and lays out a print-ready PDF. Every guide in the library comes off the same line.

Idea

Screenshots of settings screens are how parents actually learn this, and every guide online is a wall of prose. Annotate the real screen and the instruction disappears into the picture.

Brand

One template: black phone frame, a pink box on the exact thing to tap, step numbers, attribution footer. The pink box is not decoration, it is the instruction.

Build

A folder of raw phone screenshots goes in, branded annotated PDFs come out. The redaction and tap-ring steps are fragile, so every output is checked by eye before it ships.

Role
Built the pipeline and shot every screenshot. No stock, no borrowed assets, all first-party.
Approach
Scripted image composition and layout, then automated publishing to the download site and the email templates that deliver them.
Result
Eight published guides and over 400 framed captures that regenerate from source instead of being rebuilt by hand. The template family is the product, not any single guide.

Agent tooling

Carousel and publishing system

I write a topic on the command line and get back a finished, on-brand carousel, then publish it straight to the channel without opening a browser. Brand-locked template families rather than bespoke posts, because ten posts that ship well beat one that is perfect and late. Two separate brand identities run through the same machine without ever bleeding into each other.

Idea

The reason good organisations post badly is that every post needs a designer. Take the designer out of the loop without taking the design out of the post.

Brand

Seven slide types and a token system rather than per-post design. Constraints are what make an unattended feed stay on brand.

Build

A JSON file goes in, headless Chrome renders brand-correct 1080x1350 frames, and they post as a native Instagram carousel and a LinkedIn multi-image. No Canva, no Buffer, no designer.

Role
Designed the templates and built the generator, the scheduler and the publishing layer.
Approach
Model-driven layout against fixed brand constraints, published through the platform APIs directly, with per-brand credentials kept apart.
Result
A running creator practice under my own name, publishing on a schedule rather than in bursts.

Video and audio

Reels and the transcribe pipeline

A short-form video series about things I have actually built, written to a fixed script structure so the series holds together instead of drifting. Underneath it, a command that takes any video URL and returns a clean, structured transcript, which is how research turns into scripts without a manual step in between.

Idea

There is already a camera on the speaker stand at every gig. The footage is not the problem. Finding the six good minutes inside two hours is the problem.

Brand

Vertical, captioned, cut on the beat, and consistent enough that a feed of them reads as one thing rather than a folder of clips.

Build

Energy analysis finds the song boundaries in an unattended two hour recording and cuts it into publishable reels. One of them has 57,288 views.

Role
Wrote the script framework, shot and cut the series, built the transcription tool.
Approach
Local speech-to-text so nothing leaves the machine, then a model pass to structure the output into something a writer can use.
Result
Research to publishable script in one command. As generative tools spread, the differentiator is craft and judgment rather than raw output, so every script carries one deliberate design decision.

Measurement

Social metrics tracker

Pulls per-post numbers from the scheduler into a flat data file and renders a local report. It reports medians rather than averages, on purpose: reach on a personal account is lottery-shaped, a handful of posts carry twenty times the typical one, and a mean describes none of them. The median says what a normal post does. A separate count says how often the lottery pays.

Role
Built the sync, the data contract and the report.
Approach
Data file as the source of truth, the view generated from it, so the report rebuilds instead of being maintained.
Result
Told me which register works on which platform. That finding now governs what gets posted where, and it overturned what I assumed.

  • Buffer API
  • Generated HTML
  • JSON contracts
Research tool

Job Scout

Built in an afternoon. It reads every open posting across twenty company job boards, throws out anything hybrid, on-site or junior before scoring, ranks what survives against a tuned term model, and renders a report. Search engines block automation, so it goes to the applicant tracking systems directly, where the full job description is available and nothing gets rate-limited.

Role
Designed the gates and the scoring model, built the fetcher and the report.
Approach
Deterministic scoring with an explainable breakdown per posting, so a result can always be interrogated rather than trusted.
Result
1,783 postings read, 83 kept. The hybrid filter alone removed 573. It independently surfaced the two roles I had already found by hand, in the top six.
  • Node
  • Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby APIs
  • Scoring model
  • Generated HTML

The part software cannot do

The thread through all of it is the same, and it is not the code. It is taking something complicated or frightening and making a person who is not an expert know exactly what to do next. Tools keep getting better at producing output. They are not getting better at knowing which output was worth making, or at earning the trust required for someone to act on it.

That is the work I want to keep doing, and I would rather show it than describe it.
