Selected work

Four, in depth

Same four moves every time. Find the source of truth and get it into a table. Make the template a data object so a row can become a frame. Generate against it. Publish unattended. The projects below are that method pointed at four different problems, and the fourth one is not social media at all.

Generative media SaaS

AdvisorAssist

On-brand social content, generated and published for every customer on a schedule, for financial advisors across Canada. Built in 2022, before there was a name for it, and it ran live.

01
The source

They buy a tier and connect their own accounts

Stripe checkout writes the plan onto an Airtable row. A single sign-on handoff through Ayrshare returns a profile key, so the advisor links their own LinkedIn, Facebook and Instagram and I never hold a password. That row is the source of truth from then on: plan, platforms, institution, hashtags, brand colours.

02
The generation

A year of posts, written before the year starts

Two years of my partner's own posts became the voice corpus, mapped to the seasons that move an advisor's business, then generated as a full library through the OpenAI API called from inside Airtable. Batch, not per post. The runtime reads from a library rather than waiting on a model, which is what made it cheap enough to run monthly for everyone.

03
The template

Their brand, composited in, four ways

In BannerBear the post is a template with named layers, so the advisor's own logo and their own three brand colours drop into one of four designs. Hashtags assemble by tier in code: Engage gets the basics, Elevate adds their institution, Elite adds their custom set.

04
The publish

Once a month, unattended

A scheduled emitter wakes at 12:01, reads the customer view, and fires one event per advisor carrying their key and their settings. $.send.emit(object). A listener assembles that advisor's month and POSTs to Ayrshare with the scheduled date on it. Nobody looks at it.

Role
Built the product. A partner brought the advisor relationships.
Decision
Copy was generated in batch, against a calendar, rather than per post at publish time. It cost a month of lead time and it is why the pipeline could run for every advisor every month without a model in the hot path.
Status
Ran live. Wound down when the partnership stalled, not when the product did. The publish workflow reached v341.

Two accounts, one pipeline

Wines and Tours of the OkanaganWinesTours

Two Instagram accounts running the same machine on different data. Five post series off one table, then the whole thing pointed at a second source and run again.

01
The source

The winery database, read through its API

The provincial winery database is public and has an API. Read it into Airtable, one row per wine: producer, vintage, varietal, the five tasting measures, the account handle, the bottle shot. From there the content problem is a rendering problem.

02
The template

Twenty-eight named layers, not artwork

The card is a data object: wine_name, value_tannins, meter_acidity, colour_colour, wine_image. Meter bar length and the colour swatch are computed from the row. That is the whole trick, and it is why a new series was a new template rather than a new build.

03
The generation

Five series over the same table

Wine profile cards, award scores off the national wine awards, tasting notes, regional facts, and a weekly winery feature. Same rows, five templates, four visual registers that still read as one account. Winery events came from somewhere else again, parsed out of an inbox.

04
The publish

On a schedule, then again somewhere else

Make.com runs the cycle and publishes through the Instagram Graph API with nobody in the loop. Then the same rendering system got pointed at wine tour reviews and ran a second account. A configuration, not a rebuild, which is the only reason one person could run two feeds.

Role
Everything. Brand, scraper, templates, generation, scheduling, both accounts.
Decision
Every field on the card is a named layer rather than baked into the artwork. It costs more up front and it is the reason pointing the machine at a second data source was a configuration rather than a rebuild.

Geospatial product

Lake Country Zoning

Sizing a project against a lot means reading a bylaw, finding the zone, applying four different setbacks to four edges, working out which edge is even the front, subtracting any overlays, then deciding whether coverage or geometry binds. Half an hour, every time. This does it from an address in about fifteen seconds.

01
The source

An address becomes a polygon

BC Geocoder resolves the address, ParcelMap BC returns the authoritative parcel geometry over WFS, and the District's ArcGIS is the fallback. A point query on the centroid gives the zone, then five more layers for development permit overlays. Everything works in EPSG:3005 so areas come out in real metres.

02
The rules

Read the bylaw once, not on every request

Bylaw 561 is a PDF. A regex extractor parses every zone's setbacks out of it into zone_setbacks.json, ahead of time. This is the decision the product rests on. Seconds instead of tens of seconds, nothing per request, and it gives up handling a bylaw written in an unusual format. Zones that will not parse return null with a warning rather than a confident wrong number.

03
The geometry

Work out which edge is the front

The hard part, and it is not a data problem. Setbacks differ per edge, so the geometry has to know which edge is which. Road centrelines within 60 metres, front is the nearest edge inside 25, flanking is the second road edge sharing a corner, rear is farthest from front, the rest is interior. Irregular lots still catch it out, so the output says when it fell back to a heuristic.

04
The render

Intersect the half-planes and draw the answer

Each edge inset by its own setback is an inward half-plane. Intersect them and what remains is the buildable envelope. Shapely for geometry, Matplotlib and contextily for the map, and the result states whether lot coverage or the geometry is binding.

Role
Everything. Built for an architectural design practice, whose principal is the primary user.
Decision
The bylaw is parsed once into JSON, not read by a model at request time. It made the tool answer in seconds instead of tens of seconds, cost nothing to run, and gave up the ability to handle a bylaw written in an unusual format. That trade is why it is a product rather than a demo.
Honest gap
Easements have no public endpoint, so the tool says so rather than quietly returning a number that is too big. Same for flag lots, where front-edge detection can misclassify and the output warns.

Brand, site, product

Safe Family Internet

A whole product built to get non-technical parents to change one thing at home tonight. Identity, site, a gated library of step-by-step guides, and the publishing engine behind all of it.

01
The source

One model, and everything hangs off it

Parents are not short of warnings. They are short of one thing to do tonight. So everything is organised around four layers, apps, devices, network and community, in the order you should fix them. Every guide, slide and post is a step inside one of those rings.

02
The template

An identity a volunteer can use without me

A shield, a wordmark, and a mark that animates from unprotected to protected, exported as a full matrix so nobody has to crop anything. Then a guide template: black phone frame, brand pink on the exact control to tap, numbered steps.

03
The generation

Raw screenshots in, branded frames out

A folder of phone screenshots becomes eight annotated PDF guides. Separately, a JSON file becomes brand-correct 1080x1350 carousel frames, rendered by headless Chrome straight off the design tokens. Neither step has a designer in it.

04
The publish

Straight to the platforms, and a gate behind it

Native Instagram carousels through the Graph API and multi-image posts through the LinkedIn REST API. No Canva, no scheduler. The guides sit behind a serverless function that validates email and consent, adds the address to the campaign platform, and sends the toolkit.

Role
Everything. Brand, copy, design, build, deploy, and the talk it all supports.
Result
260 sign-ups claimed every seat, 38 more on the waitlist, and 48 households ran a home network checkup. Covered by CBC Daybreak and AM1150.

The part software cannot do

The thread through all of it is the same, and it is not the code. It is taking something complicated or frightening and making a person who is not an expert know exactly what to do next. Tools keep getting better at producing output. They are not getting better at knowing which output was worth making, or at earning the trust required for someone to act on it.

That is the work I want to keep doing, and I would rather show it than describe it.
