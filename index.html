AdvisorAssist

On-brand social content, generated and published for every customer on a schedule, for financial advisors across Canada. An advisor paid through Stripe, connected their own accounts, and picked a tier. From there it ran itself: a scheduled emitter pulled that tier out of Airtable and fired one event per advisor, a listener assembled the post, composited it into a template using their own logo and their own three brand colours, and published it to their connected accounts on their scheduled date. Built in 2022, before there was a name for any of it.

Idea

An advisor's whole business is trust, and the thing that builds it is showing up in the feed every week. Almost none of them do, because writing is not the job they trained for. Sell them the showing up.

Brand

Named, drawn and priced. Logo, tagline, and three tiers, Engage, Elevate, Elite, each with its own rendered banner, because a tier nobody can picture does not sell.

Build

A scheduled emitter fires one event per advisor. Listeners assemble the post, composite it into a template with that advisor's own logo and brand colours, and publish through a webhook. v341.

Role
Built the product. A partner brought the advisor relationships.
Pipeline
Emitter and listener workflows on a monthly schedule, tier-gated features in code, per-advisor auth by single sign-on so nobody handed me a password, and a branded render step before publish.
The copy
Generated in batch, not per post. Two years of my partner's own writing as the voice, mapped to the seasons that move an advisor's year, run through the OpenAI API from inside Airtable.
Status
Ran live. Wound down when the partnership stalled, not when the product did. The publish workflow reached v341.

Wines and Tours of the Okanagan

Two Instagram accounts running the same machine on different data. The wine one scraped the provincial winery database through its API and turned rows into posts: tasting notes, award scores off the national wine awards, regional facts, and a weekly winery feature. It also read winery events out of an inbox and posted those. Then the same machine got pointed at wine tour reviews and ran a second account. Four post series and two accounts off one rendering system is the part I care about. None of it was a rebuild.

Idea

Every winery in the province is already in a public database, and every one of them wants to be found. Nobody was turning that into a feed. So the source data became the content, and the only real question left was the rendering.

Brand

A mark, a wordmark, a gradient set and a headline voice. Four post series that look nothing like each other and still read as one account, which is the only way an unattended feed holds together.

Build

The template behind the profile card, as data. Twenty-eight named layers so a row becomes a frame: wine_name, value_tannins, meter_acidity, colour_colour, wine_image. Airtable in, rendered post out, published on a schedule.

Role
Everything. Brand, scraper, templates, generation, scheduling, both accounts.
Series
Wine profile cards, award scores, tasting notes, regional facts, a weekly winery feature, and winery events pulled from an inbox. One system, five templates.
The good one
The profile card renders five tasting measures as meters, bar length and colour swatch both computed from the row. Structured data becoming a designed frame, which is the whole job.
Approach
Structured source data in, generated on-brand posts out, published on a schedule with nobody in the loop. A template family rather than a run of one-offs.
Decision
Every field on the card is a named layer rather than baked into the artwork. It costs more up front and it is the reason pointing the machine at a second data source was a configuration rather than a rebuild.

Geospatial product

Lake Country Zoning

A designer sizing a project against a lot has to read a zoning bylaw, find the right zone, apply four different setbacks to four edges, work out which edge is even the front, subtract any development permit overlays, and then decide whether lot coverage or geometry is the binding constraint. It takes half an hour and it is the same half hour every time. This does it from an address.

Idea

The bylaw is already public and the parcel data is already public. Nobody had put them in the same sentence. The work was never getting the data, it was deciding which edge of an irregular lot counts as the front.

Brand

One field and one button. Everything hard is behind it. A professional tool used between meetings earns nothing by looking like a dashboard.

Build

Parcel polygon from ParcelMap BC, zone from the District's GIS, setbacks regex-extracted from the Bylaw 561 PDF, overlays from five more endpoints, then the envelope as an intersection of inward half-planes. FastAPI and GeoPandas on Railway, Next.js on Vercel.

Role
Everything. Built for an architectural design practice, whose principal is the primary user.
Decision
The bylaw is parsed once into JSON, not read by a model at request time. It made the tool answer in seconds instead of tens of seconds, cost nothing to run, and gave up the ability to handle a bylaw written in an unusual format. That trade is why it is a product rather than a demo.
Honest gap
Easements have no public endpoint, so the tool says so rather than quietly returning a number that is too big. Same for flag lots, where front-edge detection can misclassify and the output warns.

Safe Family Internet

A whole product built to get non-technical parents to actually change something at home. Identity, the live site, a gated download library of step-by-step guides, and the email pipeline behind it. The hard part was never the technology. It was making a frightening subject simple enough that a tired parent would act on it the same evening.

Idea

Parents are not short of warnings about the internet. They are short of one thing they can actually do tonight. So the whole product answers a single question: what do I change, and in what order.

Brand

A shield, a wordmark, and a mark that animates from unprotected to protected. Built so a volunteer could use it without me in the room.

Build

A static site on continuous deploy, a gated download library, and an email gate through a serverless function. 260 sign-ups claimed every seat for one night in a town of 15,000.

Role
Everything. Brand, copy, design, build, deploy, and the talk it all supports.
Approach
Static site on a private repo, continuous deploy, an email gate wired through a serverless function into the campaign platform and its templates.
Result
260 sign-ups claimed every seat, 38 more on the waitlist, and 48 households ran a home network checkup. Covered by CBC Daybreak and AM1150.

The part software cannot do

The thread through all of it is the same, and it is not the code. It is taking something complicated or frightening and making a person who is not an expert know exactly what to do next. Tools keep getting better at producing output. They are not getting better at knowing which output was worth making, or at earning the trust required for someone to act on it.

That is the work I want to keep doing, and I would rather show it than describe it.
