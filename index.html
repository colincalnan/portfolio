Generative media SaaS

AdvisorAssist

On-brand social content, generated and published for every customer on a schedule, for financial advisors across Canada. An advisor paid through Stripe, connected their own accounts, and picked a tier. From there it ran itself: a scheduled emitter pulled that tier out of Airtable and fired one event per advisor, a listener assembled the post, composited it into a template using their own logo and their own three brand colours, and published it to their connected accounts on their scheduled date. Built in 2022, before there was a name for any of it.

01

They buy a tier and connect their own accounts

Stripe checkout writes the plan onto an Airtable row. Then a single sign-on handoff through Ayrshare gives back a profile key, so the advisor links their own LinkedIn, Facebook and Instagram and I never hold a password. A separate workflow polls until that profile actually exists, because nothing downstream can run without the key.

Three tiers, three rendered banners. Engage, Elevate and Elite each unlock a different amount of the pipeline, and the difference is enforced in code rather than in a sales conversation.

02

A year of posts, written before the year starts

Two years of my partner's own posts became the voice corpus. That got mapped to the seasons that actually move an advisor's business, tax season, the new year, the RRSP deadline, and a full library was generated against that calendar through the OpenAI API, called from inside Airtable. Generation was batch, not per post: the pipeline reads from a library rather than calling a model at publish time.

03

Once a month, fan out one event per advisor

A scheduled emitter wakes at 12:01, reads the customer view, and builds each advisor's platform list from which accounts they actually connected. Then one event each, carrying their profile key, plan, institution and hashtag sets. $.send.emit(object).

Five Pipedream workflows: the emitter, two listeners, a JWT generator and a profile guard. The publish listener reached v341.

04

Composite their brand onto it, then publish

The listener pulls that advisor's month of content, picks one of four BannerBear templates, and injects their logo and their three brand colours. Hashtags assemble by tier: Engage gets the basics, Elevate adds their institution, Elite adds their custom set. Then a POST to Ayrshare with the scheduled date on it, and the post appears on their feed on the day it was meant to.

Role
Built the product. A partner brought the advisor relationships.
Pipeline
Emitter and listener workflows on a monthly schedule, tier-gated features in code, per-advisor auth by single sign-on so nobody handed me a password, and a branded render step before publish.
The copy
Generated in batch, not per post. Two years of my partner's own writing as the voice, mapped to the seasons that move an advisor's year, run through the OpenAI API from inside Airtable.
Status
Ran live. Wound down when the partnership stalled, not when the product did. The publish workflow reached v341.

Two accounts, one pipeline

Wines and Tours of the Okanagan

Two Instagram accounts running the same machine on different data. The wine one scraped the provincial winery database through its API and turned rows into posts: tasting notes, award scores off the national wine awards, regional facts, and a weekly winery feature. It also read winery events out of an inbox and posted those. Then the same machine got pointed at wine tour reviews and ran a second account. Four post series and two accounts off one rendering system is the part I care about. None of it was a rebuild.

01

Take the winery database and make it a table

The provincial winery database is public and has an API. Read it into Airtable, one row per wine: producer, vintage, varietal, the tasting measures, the account handle, the bottle shot. From that point the content problem is a rendering problem.

02

Turn one table into five different post series

Wine profile cards, award scores off the national wine awards, tasting notes, regional facts, and a weekly winery feature. Five templates reading the same rows. Winery events came from a different source again, parsed out of an inbox.

03

Make the template a data object, not artwork

In BannerBear the card is 28 named layers: wine_name, value_tannins, meter_acidity, colour_colour, wine_image. The meter bar length and the colour swatch are both computed from the row. That is the whole trick, and it is why a new series was a new template rather than a new build.

The BannerBear editor. Every field on the card is addressable, so a row becomes a frame.

04

Schedule it, then point the same machine somewhere else

Make.com runs the cycle and publishes through the Instagram Graph API on a schedule with nobody in the loop. Then the same rendering system got pointed at wine tour reviews and ran a second account. That was a configuration, not a rebuild, which is the only reason a one-person operation could run two feeds.

Everything. Brand, scraper, templates, generation, scheduling, both accounts.

Geospatial product

Lake Country Zoning

A designer sizing a project against a lot has to read a zoning bylaw, find the right zone, apply four different setbacks to four edges, work out which edge is even the front, subtract any development permit overlays, and then decide whether lot coverage or geometry is the binding constraint. It takes half an hour and it is the same half hour every time. This does it from an address.

01

Turn an address into a polygon

BC Geocoder resolves the address, then ParcelMap BC gives the authoritative parcel geometry over WFS, with the District's own ArcGIS as a fallback. Everything downstream works in EPSG:3005, BC Albers, so areas come out in real metres instead of degrees.

02

Ask the District what the lot actually is

A point query on the parcel centroid against the zoning FeatureServer returns the zone code. Then five more layers for development permit overlays: natural environment, hillside, stability, erosion, drainage. Anything that overlaps gets subtracted from the buildable area later.

03

Read the bylaw once, not on every request

Bylaw 561 is a PDF. A regex extractor parses every zone's setbacks out of it into zone_setbacks.json, ahead of time. This is the decision the product rests on. It answers in seconds instead of tens of seconds, costs nothing per request, and gives up the ability to handle a bylaw written in an unusual format. Zones that do not parse cleanly return null with a warning rather than a confident wrong number.

04

Work out which edge is the front

This is the hard part, and it is not a data problem. Setbacks differ per edge, so the geometry has to know which edge is which. Road centrelines within 60 metres, front is the nearest edge inside a 25 metre threshold, flanking is the second road-adjacent edge sharing a corner with the front, rear is the edge farthest from the front, everything else is interior. Irregular lots still catch it out, so the output says when it fell back to a heuristic.

05

Intersect the half-planes and draw the answer

Each edge inset by its own setback becomes an inward half-plane. Intersect all of them and what is left is the buildable envelope. Shapely does the geometry, Matplotlib and contextily render the map, and the result says whether lot coverage or the geometry is the binding constraint.

The generated site plan. Parcel in black, envelope in green, every setback drawn and labelled, with the areas computed underneath.

Role
Everything. Built for an architectural design practice, whose principal is the primary user.
Decision
The bylaw is parsed once into JSON, not read by a model at request time. It made the tool answer in seconds instead of tens of seconds, cost nothing to run, and gave up the ability to handle a bylaw written in an unusual format. That trade is why it is a product rather than a demo.
Honest gap
Easements have no public endpoint, so the tool says so rather than quietly returning a number that is too big. Same for flag lots, where front-edge detection can misclassify and the output warns.

Brand, site, product

Safe Family Internet

A whole product built to get non-technical parents to actually change something at home. Identity, the live site, a gated download library of step-by-step guides, and the email pipeline behind it. The hard part was never the technology. It was making a frightening subject simple enough that a tired parent would act on it the same evening.

01

Pick one question and answer only that

Parents are not short of warnings. They are short of one thing to do tonight. So everything got organised around a single model: four layers, apps, devices, network and community, in the order you should fix them. Every guide, slide and post is a step inside one of those rings.

The castle model. It is the information architecture for the whole project, not a diagram made afterwards.

02

Build an identity a volunteer can use without me

A shield, a wordmark, and a mark that animates from unprotected to protected. Exported as a full matrix, magenta, on black, on white, primary cobalt, at five sizes each, so nobody ever has to ask which file to use or crop one themselves.

The primary mark. One of twenty-two exports covering every colourway and size the project needed.

03

Ship the site and the gate behind it

A static site on continuous deploy, and a download library gated behind a serverless function that validates the email and consent, adds the address to the campaign platform, and sends the toolkit. Eight branded PDF guides sit behind it, each built from raw phone screenshots by a pipeline rather than laid out by hand.

04

Publish it without a designer in the loop

A JSON file goes in, headless Chrome renders brand-correct 1080x1350 frames from design tokens, and they post as a native Instagram carousel and a LinkedIn multi-image. That engine is why one person could keep a campaign running.

One rendered carousel slide. The type, the highlight block and the footer all come from tokens.

Role
Everything. Brand, copy, design, build, deploy, and the talk it all supports.
Approach
Static site on a private repo, continuous deploy, an email gate wired through a serverless function into the campaign platform and its templates.
Result
260 sign-ups claimed every seat, 38 more on the waitlist, and 48 households ran a home network checkup. Covered by CBC Daybreak and AM1150.

The part software cannot do

The thread through all of it is the same, and it is not the code. It is taking something complicated or frightening and making a person who is not an expert know exactly what to do next. Tools keep getting better at producing output. They are not getting better at knowing which output was worth making, or at earning the trust required for someone to act on it.

That is the work I want to keep doing, and I would rather show it than describe it.
