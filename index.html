Three, in depth

Generative media SaaS

AdvisorAssist

A subscription that wrote and posted a financial advisor's social media for them, every month, on their own branding, without them doing anything.

Financial advisors know their clients need to hear from them every week. Almost none of them post, because writing is not the job they trained for. So we sold them the posting. They paid, connected their accounts, and their feed filled itself.

01
The source

They buy a tier and connect their own accounts

They pay through Stripe, which sets their plan. Then they link their own LinkedIn, Facebook and Instagram themselves, through a handoff that means I never see a password. Everything the system needs about them now lives on one row: what they paid for, which accounts to post to, their firm, their hashtags, their brand colours.

02
The generation

A year of posts, written before the year starts

An advisor's year has a shape: tax season, the new year, RRSP deadlines. So we took two years of my partner's own posts, fed them in as the voice to copy, and wrote a whole year of content against that calendar in one go. Written ahead, not on the day. That is what made it cheap enough to run for everybody, every month.

03
The template

Their brand, composited in, four ways

The four designs are not pictures, they are forms with slots. Their logo goes in one slot, their three brand colours in others, so every post comes out looking like their firm rather than like a template. What they paid for decides how much gets filled in.

04
The publish

Once a month, unattended

Once a month, at one minute past midnight, it wakes up, goes through the customer list, and hands each advisor off to be built and posted. Their month goes out on the days it was meant to. Nobody watches it happen.

Role
Built the product. A partner brought the advisor relationships.
Decision
Copy was generated in batch, against a calendar, rather than per post at publish time. It cost a month of lead time and it is why the pipeline could run for every advisor every month without a model in the hot path.
Status
Ran live, posting to advisors' own feeds on a schedule.

Two accounts, one pipeline

Wines and Tours of the OkanaganWinesTours

An Instagram account that builds itself out of a public winery database, and then a second one built the same way.

Every winery in the province is listed in a public database and every one of them wants to be found. Nobody was turning that list into something people would follow. So I did, twice.

01
The source

The winery database, read through its API

The database is public, so I pulled the whole thing into a spreadsheet: one row per wine, with the producer, the vintage, how it tastes, the winery's handle and a photo of the bottle. Once the facts are in a table, the only question left is what they should look like.

02
The template

Twenty-eight named layers, not artwork

Every part of the card has a name, so the design can go and fetch its own content. The tasting notes are the good bit: the bar next to "tannins" is as long as the number in that column, and the little colour dot is the wine's actual colour. Nobody sets those. That is the whole trick, and it is why adding a new kind of post later meant making a new form, not building a new thing.

03
The generation

Five series over the same table

Five kinds of post, all off the same table: a tasting profile, an award score, a tasting note, a fact about the region, and a winery of the week. They look nothing like each other and still read as one account, which is the hard part. Winery events came from somewhere else entirely, pulled out of an inbox.

04
The publish

On a schedule, then again somewhere else

It posts itself, on a schedule, with nobody approving anything. Then I pointed the same setup at wine tour reviews and got a second account out of it. That was an afternoon of settings, not a rebuild, which is the only reason one person could run two feeds at once.

Role
Everything. Brand, scraper, templates, generation, scheduling, both accounts.
Decision
Every field on the card is a named layer rather than baked into the artwork. It costs more up front and it is the reason pointing the machine at a second data source was a configuration rather than a rebuild.

Brand, site, product

Safe Family InternetSiteGuides

A brand, a set of guides and the machine that produces them, made to get parents to change one thing at home tonight.

An internet safety initiative I started in Lake Country. Parents are told constantly that the internet is dangerous for their kids and almost nobody tells them what to change, or in what order. So I built the answer, and then I built the brand that carries it, so other people could use it without me in the room.

01
The idea

One model, and everything hangs off it

Everything hangs off one idea: there are four layers between a stranger and your kid, and you should fix them in order. The apps, then the device, then the home network, then the people around them. Every guide, every slide and every post is one step inside one of those four.

02
The template

A brand, and the rules for using it

A shield, a name, and a logo that animates from unprotected to protected, exported as 22 files covering every colourway and size so a volunteer never has to crop one. Under it a design system: the colours, the type, and seven kinds of slide. The deck, the guides, the posts and the site all read from it.

03
The generation

Everything comes out on brand without a designer

Take screenshots on your phone, drop the folder in, and out come eight branded step-by-step guides, each screen framed with a pink box around the thing to tap. Posts work the same way: type the words, get finished slides in the right colours and fonts. A roughly 200 slide deck came off the same system.

04
The publish

Out to the platforms, and into people's homes

The slides post straight to Instagram and LinkedIn as proper swipeable posts, with no Canva and no scheduler in between. The guides sit behind an email form that checks the address, adds it to the list and sends the download. 260 sign-ups claimed every seat for one night in a town of 15,000.

Role
Everything. Brand, copy, design, build, deploy, and the talk it all supports.
Result
260 sign-ups claimed every seat, 38 more on the waitlist, and 48 households ran a home network checkup. Covered by CBC Daybreak and AM1150.

Craft

Things I keep building

Geospatial tool

Lake Country Zoning

Before a designer can sketch anything they need to know how much of the lot they can build on, which means reading the zoning bylaw and measuring in from every edge by a different amount. Half an hour, every time. Type an address into this and it comes back in fifteen seconds, drawn on the lot.

Product, with payments

Set List Easy

You know six chords and you have a two hour gig on Friday. Every song list online is sorted by artist or decade, never by how hard it is to play. So this one is. People pay for it.

Live tool, on stage

Gig PulseAppSite

Every musician thinks they know which songs work. Nobody writes it down, because you cannot take notes with a guitar on. So this is one tap: applause is worth one, dancing two, singing three, screaming five. After enough gigs it tells you which songs to keep. 120 songs scored so far.

Public writing

Loops

Getting an agent to finish a job instead of stopping halfway is a skill, and almost nobody writes it down. So I did: fifteen free lessons on designing agent loops, what makes a check real rather than two optimists agreeing, and where to keep a person in the chair. It is how I work every day, in Claude Code and Codex.

Design system

Bold Talks, and Ground

I kept redrawing the same slides. So the colours and type became a file, and now the deck, the guides and this page all read from it. It also ships a checker that refuses to publish a page that breaks the rules.

Publishing engine

Carousel and publishing

Type the words, get back finished slides in the right colours and fonts, posted straight to Instagram and LinkedIn as proper swipeable posts. No Canva, no scheduling tool, no designer.

Video pipeline

Reels and transcribe

There is already a phone on the speaker stand at every gig I play, recording the whole thing. This listens to the two hour file, works out where each song starts and stops from how loud it is, and cuts them into captioned clips. That one gig came out as 23 songs. One of the clips has 57,288 views.

Agent loop

Job Scout

Job boards are mostly noise. This reads 4,130 postings a week, throws out anything that fails a fact I can check by machine, then has a model read what is left against a description of what I actually want, written in plain English. It found this job.

Instrumentation

Social metrics

Pulls the numbers back out of everything I post and tells me the truth, including the unwelcome part: reach behaves like a lottery, so the useful question is how often you buy a ticket.

The part software cannot do

The thread through all of it is the same, and it is not the code. It is taking something complicated or frightening and making a person who is not an expert know exactly what to do next. Tools keep getting better at producing output. They are not getting better at knowing which output was worth making, or at earning the trust required for someone to act on it.

That is the work I want to keep doing, and I would rather show it than describe it.
